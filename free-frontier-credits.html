Who actually gives away frontier models

A verified survey of free credits for Opus 5, GPT‑5.6 and Gemini 3 — what is real money, what is a coupon on someone else's account, and what the difference costs you.

The short answer

Your premise is half right. AgentRouter is real, it is live, and it genuinely serves claude-opus-5, claude-opus-4-8 and gpt-5.6-sol. But the $175 is not a signup grant — it is $50 signup + $150 per referral (raised from $100), and the credits are internal quota units, not dollars. You cannot pay them even if you want to: top‑up is disabled.

The wider finding is the one worth acting on. There are two completely separate economies here, and mixing them up is the expensive mistake. First‑party grants are real money with real terms. Relay gateways — the Chinese 中转站 ecosystem your question points at — are cheap because many resell reverse‑engineered subscription access, and they can read every prompt you send.

Two things that changed under everyone's feet Verified

  • GitHub Models is dead. Fully retired 30 July 2026 — playground, catalog, inference API and BYOK all gone for every customer. It was the standard recommendation until weeks ago.
  • GitHub Student Pack lost Copilot Pro. Replaced by a weaker "Copilot Student" tier with auto model selection only — students cannot pick Opus 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro or GPT‑5.6, and get no API.

The cloud‑trial myth Verified

The widely repeated belief that a cloud free trial gets you free frontier Claude is false on both major clouds. Google's $300/90‑day credit explicitly excludes generative‑AI partner models offered as managed APIs, and Azure's Claude offering is postpaid Claude Consumption Units with no prepaid credits. Free frontier Claude comes from Antigravity or Claude.ai — not from cloud credits.

Tier 03 · Relay gateways · the ecosystem you asked about

The 中转站 economy

Roughly 150 domains were probed directly. Most run the same open‑source New API panel, whose backend endpoints answer without authentication — so the numbers below come from the operators' own configs, not their marketing.

AgentRouter, decoded from its own backend Verified

Its config exposes quota_per_unit: 500000, so quota converts cleanly: quota_for_invitee: 25000000 = $50 signup, quota_for_inviter: 75000000 = $150 per referral, plus a $25 daily check‑in. Your $175 is most likely $50 + $100 referral + $25 check‑in accumulated, not a lump grant. Models confirmed live: claude-opus-5 (ratio 1.0), claude-opus-4-8 (1.5), gpt-5.6-sol (2.0). Signup takes GitHub or linux.do OAuth with no email verification.

本站目标还是服务于开发者，而非提供稳定的大模型调用服务，如有诉求，建议直接前往 openrouter 或各家模型官方。 "Our goal is serving developers, not providing a stable LLM service. If you need that, go to OpenRouter or the official providers." — AgentRouter's own announcement

The same archive records Claude models being mass‑disabled in May 2026, the site being banned by its upstream in September 2025 after users generated NSFW content, and an October 2025 outage spent "looking for a new upstream." A documented 400 content blocked error fires when your prompt is not in Chinese, English, French, German or Russian — which means prompts are inspected.

What free credit actually exists across the ecosystem Verified

Community lists advertise large signup bonuses. Probing the panels directly refuted most of them:

  • apidock.ai — 新用户注册即送 $1 体验金: a confirmed but small $1 automatic signup grant.
  • api.aisz.mom — the real figure is $0.1688, not the $1 claimed.
  • newapi.makelove.cloud — the advertised $15 signup is cancelled by the operator's own notice: 「取消要求新用户免费送额度机制（遭不住了）」 — "cancelled, we can't sustain it."
  • Daily check‑in is live on only 8 of ~50 probed panels, and no panel exposes the amount without an account.
  • bluebirdapi.com, nova.vcrauo.com — referral‑only. Signing up alone grants nothing.

Meanwhile several sites list claude-opus-5 at ratio 37.5 — an unconfigured placeholder equal to ~$75/M input. Those listings are decorative; the model is not really priced or served.

Why it is cheap — in the operators' own words Verified

Two independent sites label their own supply. yunwu.ai names its channel tiers including 逆向 — reverse‑engineered — sold alongside "official‑transit" and "direct" tiers. nexaxis.io goes further, naming groups Claude_Kiro逆向, ChatGPT_plus逆向 and Gemini_cli反代 (reverse proxy). The supporting tooling — what the surrounding ecosystem is built from
  • Subscription wrappers. A ~48k‑star project wraps Antigravity, Codex, Claude Code and Grok subscriptions into API endpoints, advertising "free Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT 5.6, Grok 4.5, Claude through API."
  • Account pools with explicit ban evasion. One repo states its OAuth pool exists to 有效规避封号 — "effectively evade account bans."
  • Mass registration. A 979‑star tool batch‑registers up to 1,000 accounts across 50 threads, explicitly to 规避风控 — "evade risk control."
  • Contribute‑to‑use key pools. One requires you to submit your own Gemini key, then round‑robins requests across everyone's keys — "possibly yours, possibly someone else's."
  • Leaked‑key scanners. Public tools scan GitHub for exposed OpenAI and Anthropic keys and "verify active secrets instantly" — a plausible supply side for some pools.

A trap to know about

Sakura exposes an Anthropic‑compatible endpoint usable from Claude Code — but it serves Kimi-K2.6, not Claude. Several relays similarly present Anthropic‑shaped APIs that do not carry Anthropic models.

The :free namespace illusion Verified · live API

openai/gpt-oss-20b:free and google/gemma-4-31b-it:free look like free GPT and Gemini. They are not — they are open‑weight releases that merely sit under the openai/ and google/ namespaces. Checking each model's hugging_face_id separates them cleanly: published weights means it is not a frontier closed model.

OpenRouter's free limits, read from its own docs constants: 20 requests/min always, 50/day under $10 lifetime credit purchases, 1,000/day at $10+. Extra accounts don't help — capacity is governed globally.

Aggregator free tiers have tightened sharply Verified

Several widely repeated recommendations no longer hold. Verified against live pricing and docs pages:

  • Together AI — "does not currently offer free trials"; a $5 minimum purchase is required to reach the platform. The old $1 signup credit is gone.
  • Cerebras — $5 in credits, a verified payment method is required, credits expire in 30 days, and asked directly whether a permanently free tier exists, the answer is "No." Public endpoints now carry two open‑weight models.
  • Groq — Llama chat models are gone from the free plan. What remains is capped at 8K tokens/min, roughly one moderate‑context request per minute.
  • Moonshot / Kimi — no free tier; you must recharge $1 before any use.
  • Alibaba Model Studio — still ~1M free tokens per model, but now 90‑day expiry and Singapore‑region only.
  • Hyperbolic — pivoted to GPU rental; no free inference tier advertised.

Still genuinely free and durable: Z.ai's GLM‑4.7‑Flash, 4.5‑Flash and 4.6V‑Flash ($0, no stated expiry), Mistral's $10/month in API credits, and Cohere's 1,000 calls/month trial keys. All open‑weight or first‑party — none carry Claude, GPT or Gemini.

Coding plans that substitute models behind Claude Code Verified

Z.ai's GLM Coding Plan (from $18/month), MiniMax and Moonshot all advertise compatibility with Claude Code, Cline and Cursor. Read carefully: these substitute their own models behind the Claude Code client. It is not Claude access, and the same is true of the Anthropic‑format endpoints offered by DeepSeek and Alibaba — API shape compatibility, not Anthropic models.

Bottom line

What I would actually do

For real work: Google Antigravity's $0 tier is the best free frontier access that exists right now — Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.6, from the vendor, with no provenance questions. Pair it with the Gemini API free tier for scripted work, and Azure for Students if you qualify.

On AgentRouter and its peers: they work, and for throwaway experiments the cost of being wrong is low. But treat every prompt as logged by a third party, never send credentials, private code or production data, expect the service to vanish or lose its upstream without notice, and don't build anything you care about on a key that a provider can revoke upstream at any moment. If it matters, it belongs on a first‑party key.

If you'd rather just pay a little: OpenRouter's pay‑as‑you‑go passthrough adds no inference markup (only a fee when buying credits), which makes it the cheapest legitimate route to frontier models. google/gemini-3.7-flash at $0.375/$1.875 per M was the cheapest frontier‑branded option found, and its :batch variant is half that. A few dollars there buys more usable, durable capacity than any relay grant in this report.
